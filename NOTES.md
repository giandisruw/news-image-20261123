### 1. Ukraine sinks Russian warship Merkury in Libya (LEAD)
- Night of Sun 22 Nov: Ukrainian forces struck two Russian vessels near
  Benghazi, Libya. Geolocated footage published 22 Nov shows fire and
  secondary explosions on the Russian Project 20380 Merkury
  Steregushchiy-class corvette docked at the Benghazi Naval Base;
  satellite imagery published 22 Nov shows most of the corvette capsized
  and sunk.
- Libyan military officials affiliated with the Russian-affiliated Libyan
  National Army said Ukrainian forces launched six unmanned sea vehicles
  (USVs) against Merkury, with two striking the corvette, from a base
  Ukrainian forces operated from in western Libya.
- Local Libyan media posted footage on 22 Nov showing Ukrainian USVs
  striking a Russian military-affiliated, Maltese-flagged Ascalon cargo
  ship off the coast of Benghazi.
- Sources: ISW Russian Offensive Campaign Assessment 22 Nov, h.ua
  (22 Nov), The True Story world round-up (23 Nov), vesseltracker
  maritime insights (22 Nov).

### 2. COP31 collapses with no deal
- Sun 22 Nov, Antalya, Turkey: COP31 ended without an overarching climate
  agreement after two weeks of talks and two days of overtime — the
  longest UN climate summit on record. The EU, UK, Panama and others
  rejected the draft Antalya Pact because it dropped all mention of
  fossil fuels; the draft also dropped a proposed $120bn adaptation
  finance target. COP31 President Murat Kurum: "I understand that there
  is no agreement on this text. For that reason, these decisions have
  not been adopted." UN Secretary-General António Guterres: "deeply
  disappointed", "there is no ambiguity in the science". COP32 will be
  in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, in 2027.
- Sources: UN News (22 Nov), World Resources Institute statement
  (22 Nov), phys.org/AP (22 Nov), IPS/Global Issues (22 Nov).

### 3. Democrats win 258 House seats
- Sun 22 Nov: Democrats secured their 258th seat in the US House,
  clinching the largest majority either party has held in the chamber
  since 1992, when North Carolina Democrat Chuck Hubbard defeated
  longtime Republican Rep. Virginia Foxx (chair of the House Rules
  Committee), per the Associated Press. Democrats have gained 44 seats
  so far (four more than in 2018); four races remain uncalled (Minnesota,
  Tennessee, two Louisiana December run-offs); Republicans hold at least
  173 seats. The Senate is also in Democratic hands, so divided
  government with a Republican president follows.
- Sources: AP via WVLT/Reflector/TMJ4 (22 Nov), Reuters (22 Nov),
  Fox News (22 Nov), CBS/AP via DailyFoxNews (22 Nov).

### 4. 14 missing after drone hit in Dymer, Kyiv region
- Sunday morning 22 Nov: a Russian drone struck a five-storey apartment
  building in Dymer, Vyshhorod district, about 45 km north of Kyiv,
  collapsing a section spanning all five floors and starting a fire.
  President Zelenskyy said 14 people remained unaccounted for (including
  two children, a 12-year-old girl and a 7-year-old boy, per regional
  police); nine people were evacuated and the fire was contained; one
  body was recovered from the rubble. Ukraine's air force said 161 of
  186 attack drones launched overnight were shot down. Zelenskyy renewed
  his call for the US to approve Patriot interceptor deliveries allies
  have already paid for, saying Russia fired about 2,700 drones, 1,460
  glide bombs and 165 missiles at Ukraine this week.
- Sources: Reuters (22 Nov), Kyiv Independent via EuropeSays (22 Nov),
  NewsNation via DailyFoxNews (22 Nov), IBTimes SG (22 Nov),
  France 24 via Vocemundi (22 Nov).

### 5. Candy factory inferno in Serang, Indonesia
- Sat 21 Nov ~17:00 local: a fire broke out at the three-storey
  PT Fonusa Agung Mulia candy factory in Cikande, Serang regency,
  Banten (maker of Milkita milk candies, part of United Family Food).
  About 300 workers were on shift; the fire started at a cooling unit
  on the third floor, survivors said, and parts of the building
  collapsed. At least three people were confirmed dead; 24 body bags
  had been removed by Sunday evening, but police warned one bag can
  hold remains of more than one person, so the death toll was unclear.
  Figures for the missing varied during Sunday (36–50 unaccounted for);
  the factory later said 29 remained missing and 8 unconfirmed. DNA
  identification from relatives' samples could take two to three weeks.
  Search was suspended Sunday evening and set to resume Monday morning.
- Sources: Reuters via Northland News Radio / 947 The Beast (22 Nov),
  AP via People (22 Nov), AFP via NAMPA (22 Nov), TBS News (22 Nov),
  Xinhua via NAMPA (22 Nov).

### 6. Sparta II towed out of Finnish waters
- Sun 22 Nov: the Russian rescue vessel Murman began towing the stricken
  Sparta II out of Finnish waters, after Finnish, Estonian and Russian
  vessels fought its fire together (seven vessels: two Russian, two
  Estonian, three Finnish). Visible flames were out; thermal cameras
  showed some containers still warm, and the ship listed slightly but
  was stable enough to tow. One crew member was slightly injured; all
  16 were evacuated. No fuel or hazardous-substance leaks were detected.
  Finnish maritime and aviation restrictions around the operation were
  lifted. The ship was hit by two drones on Sat 21 Nov in international
  waters west of Gogland island; Finland says Ukraine was likely behind
  the strike.
- Sources: Reuters via LA Post / gCaptain (22 Nov), CNN (22 Nov),
  Helsinki Times (22 Nov), Yle via FINCHANNEL (22 Nov).

### 7. South Korea education minister quits over AI cheating
- Sun 22 Nov: Education Minister Choi Kyo-jin offered his resignation
  and President Lee Jae Myung accepted it, taking responsibility for
  last-minute anti-cheating measures ahead of the Suneung (College
  Scholastic Ability Test) that caused nationwide confusion. The scandal
  began when the top scorer in a national tax accountant exam confessed
  to using a hidden miniature camera and a smartphone to feed images to
  generative AI for a near-perfect score, four days before the exam.
  Emergency measures included three nationwide disaster-alert texts,
  Wi-Fi blocking near test centres and button stickers on clothing.
  Officials found 111 cases of misconduct involving electronic devices
  on exam day.
- Sources: AFP via Inshorts / Daily AI Brief (22 Nov), Yonhap via
  Qazinform / Korea JoongAng Daily (22 Nov), The Guardian via TBS News
  (22 Nov), Aju Press (22 Nov).

### 8. Killer-robot talks stall in Geneva
- Sat 21 Nov, Geneva: after a week of talks ending at ~04:30–04:45 in
  the morning, the UN Review Conference of the Convention on Certain
  Conventional Weapons (128 parties) agreed only to "elaborate" a
  possible accord on lethal autonomous weapons systems over the next two
  years, by 2028 — no commitment to binding rules, despite the UN
  Secretary-General's 2026 deadline and pleas from the International
  Committee of the Red Cross and Pope Leo. The US, Russia and Israel
  were among those reluctant to accept binding regulations. The ICRC
  said it regretted the outcome; campaigners warned the technology will
  race ahead uncontrolled.
- Sources: AP via WCMU / Reflector (21 Nov), AFP via TechXplore
  (21 Nov).
